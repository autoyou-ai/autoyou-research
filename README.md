# Right-sizing AI at the edge — research

Two studies on whether running, and teaching, small language models on
already-owned consumer hardware actually reduces the footprint of AI inference.
Both share one parameter base, one figure language, and one standard of proof:
every number printed in either paper is regenerated by one command and checked
back against the prose.

| | **Track A — footprint & routing** | **Track B — on-device adaptation** |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Is it better to **run** a small model at home? | Is it better to **teach** one there? |
| Paper | [`paper/main.pdf`](paper/main.pdf) (14 pp) | [`paper/adaptation.pdf`](paper/adaptation.pdf) (8 pp) |
| Hypotheses | H1–H6, counter-claims C1–C3 | H7–H11, counter-claims C4–C6 |

## Track A: does running it at home help?

> *Can running small models on already-owned household hardware, reached over
> relay-confidential peer-to-peer transport, offset the carbon, water and
> data-centre footprint of cloud AI — without sacrificing privacy?*

**Yes, within a bounded regime.** For the fraction of everyday tasks a small
model handles well, against *typical or heavy* frontier cloud usage, on an
already-owned device and a reasonably clean grid: **54–64% less carbon**, on-site
cooling water nearly eliminated, **~150× cheaper per query**, and STUN/TURN
relays that forward ciphertext only.

**No** against a best-in-class efficient cloud model. Compared with Gemini's
measured 0.24 Wh/prompt, a single-user consumer GPU is on average **16% worse**,
with positive savings in only 3% of Monte-Carlo draws. The win comes from
right-sizing, from eliminating evaporative cooling and PUE/idle overhead, and
from using marginal energy on hardware that is already on — not from relocating
the same model.

### The crux variable, stated plainly

Every saving above is proportional to `f_s`, the fraction of a workload a small
model can serve. We put it at 82%. **That number is an assumption, not a
measurement.** It follows from five task-class capability ratios and five
workload weights, and the weights are not sampled from any query corpus — we did
not have one. NVIDIA's measured agentic-replaceability range for three real
systems is 40–70%, so our figure sits *above* the only external check available.

The sensitivity analysis therefore sweeps `f_s` across [0.40, 0.90] as a
first-class factor. At the midpoint of NVIDIA's band (`f_s` = 55%) carbon savings
against the GPT-4o long baseline fall from 54% to 36.5%; at its floor, to 26.5%.
The *sign* of the result survives the whole range against a frontier baseline.
The *magnitude* does not. Read your own answer off the tornado.

## Track B: does teaching it at home help?

> *Does parameter-efficient fine-tuning on already-owned hardware move `f_s` —
> and does the training run pay for itself?*

- **Feasible (H7)** — but only on the *unified-memory* device class. A 24 GB
  discrete GPU with four times the bandwidth is driven to rank 32 at sequence
  1024 before a 27B job fits; a 128 GB APU runs it at rank 128 and sequence 8192.
  Addressable memory decides feasibility; bandwidth only decides speed. The
  memory model reproduces a published 27B run to −0.6%.
- **The headline is negative (H9).** At an ordinary acceptance threshold
  (α = 0.70), adaptation moves coverage **+0.0 pp**: every adaptable class
  already cleared the bar. What improves is delivered *quality* on the work
  already served locally. Coverage responds only above α ≈ 0.80. Adaptation buys
  better answers at an ordinary quality bar, and more answers only at a
  demanding one.
- **Amortisation is accounting-dependent (H8).** A 27B run costs 10.7 kWh over
  82 hours. Against an all-cloud counterfactual that repays in 143 days at 50
  queries/day. Charged only against the routing the adapter actually changes, it
  repays nothing at α = 0.70 — because it changes none.
- **Structural privacy (H10).** Cloud fine-tuning requires the corpus to reach
  the trainer; for a persona adapter that corpus *is* the message history. A
  low-parameter adapter is 0.5–2.8 MB and crosses a datachannel in about a
  second.
- **Safety (C5), the finding worth carrying out.** Fine-tuning is neither safe
  nor unsafe: it moves whatever the corpus specifies and lets everything else
  decay. That makes the corpus, not the recipe, the safety-critical artefact.

## Primary measurement

Both papers previously borrowed every term of the edge-energy model from
third-party benchmarks and vendor TDPs. They no longer do. Measurements were
taken on two consumer machines under Ollama, single-stream, with raw per-run
records published in [`measure/results/`](measure/results):

- **AMD Radeon 8060S** (Ryzen AI MAX+ 395, 64 GB unified LPDDR5X, 256 GB/s):
  78.4 / 37.8 / 13.8 tok/s for 3.8B / 8.9B / 27.3B models; prefill factors
  0.050–0.078 from four-length regressions.
- **NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070** (12 GB GDDR7, 672 GB/s): request-window board
  energy of **0.091 Wh** (3B) and **0.19 Wh** (8B) at 499 in / 300 out, sampled
  from the board rail and bracketed, with an independent replication run.

Two significant figures is what the instrument supports: the driver refreshes
its power counter roughly every 0.4 s, so a 1.6 s request carries only four to
five *distinct* readings despite thirteen samples.

### The bandwidth ladder reports every row, including the awkward one

Nine builds were measured on the APU. Builds that stream only part of their
weights (sparse MoE, multi-token prediction, per-layer embeddings) sit above the
memory ceiling, as they must. Builds that stream all of their weights should sit
below it — and one does not, at **105.7%**.

That row is dense by the runtime's own metadata, so it cannot exceed a ceiling
it must stream under. Its excess measures the error in using `τ · W` (throughput
times on-disk size) as a proxy for bytes read per token: the file holds a vision
tower and embedding tables that decode never streams. It puts a **≥5.7 pp floor**
under the proxy's error, and it is the only calibration the statistic has.

So the ordering holds — partial readers sit above whole readers by 30 pp — but
the tight-band reading does not. The whole-weight class spans 76.7–105.7%, not a
narrow band. Two of the nine rows are builds whose weights are not
redistributable; they are marked in the figure rather than dropped, because one
of them is the row that bounds the proxy.

## Reproduce everything

```bash
python -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
cd models && python run_all.py
```

That writes `models/results.json` and regenerates all 15 figures
deterministically — the Monte-Carlo is seeded, so a second run is bit-identical.

Then check the prose against it:

```bash
cd models && python verify_paper.py
```

`run_all.py` guarantees `results.json` is internally consistent; it guarantees
nothing about whether the LaTeX quotes it faithfully. `verify_paper.py` closes
that gap by extracting every numeric token from both `.tex` files and refusing
anything it cannot trace to a measurement record, a declared parameter bound, or
an annotated citation.

It also reports how many distinct sources could have justified each number, and
prints that summary by default. A value matching many unrelated quantities is
weakly constrained by this check — it would have passed whatever the sentence
claimed. The tool shows that the prose and the records agree; it does not show
that a number sits in the right sentence.

Rebuild the papers:

```bash
cd paper && latexmk -pdf main.tex && latexmk -pdf adaptation.tex
```

Run the reference routing agent against a local model runtime:

```bash
cd agent && python demo.py
```

## Scope and limits

- **Energy figures are pinned to measured models.** No public per-query energy
  measurement covers the 2026 frontier generation or the 2026 open-weights tier.
  Pricing and architecture are current; energy, water and carbon are not, and
  are labelled as such wherever they appear.
- **The workload mix is asserted, not sampled.** See the crux-variable note
  above. It is the single largest lever in the study and the least grounded.
- **Board power is measured on one machine only.** The AMD part exposes no power
  rail readable without a kernel-mode helper the harness declines to install, so
  its energy figures still carry a modelled power term and say so at every use.
- **Board power is not wall power.** Host CPU/DRAM draw is evaluated as a
  sensitivity up to 100 W and supply conversion is charged at an assumed
  η = 0.90; the post-request idle tail is excluded, because it is a property of
  a deployment's duty cycle rather than of a query.
- **Single-stream only.** Nothing here says anything about batched serving,
  where the arithmetic intensity that makes decode bandwidth-bound no longer
  holds. Edge does not win on per-token efficiency, and the papers do not claim
  it does.
- **One adapter-evaluation input is not redistributable.** Findings depending on
  it report `NO DATA` on a public checkout rather than stale numbers, and the
  hypothesis it informs is recorded as not independently validated throughout.
